The question and the data

Three doses × two supplement methods

The experiment measured tooth length after vitamin C was delivered as orange juice (OJ) or ascorbic acid (VC), at 0.5, 1, or 2 mg/day.

App purpose: make dose-specific measurements and a cautious mean comparison easy to inspect without writing R code.

Inside the Shiny app

Two controls update the whole view

  • Choose a supplement with radio buttons.
  • Choose a dose from the three tested amounts.
  • The server reacts to both inputs: the summary, confidence interval, test, and chart update together.
  • Point the cursor at a dot to inspect an animal; the app’s left panel explains every step.

The question stays simple: what did these 10 animals show at this dose, and how uncertain is the average comparison?

What the app calculates

Pick one group; get a mean and an interval

The user selects a supplement and dose. The app calculates the selected group’s sample mean and 95% t confidence interval, then compares it with the other supplement at that dose using a Welch two-sample t-test.

example <- subset(tg, dose == 0.5)
oj <- subset(example, supp == "OJ")$len
vc <- subset(example, supp == "VC")$len
test <- t.test(oj, vc)
cat(sprintf("0.5 mg/day: OJ mean %.2f; VC mean %.2f\n", mean(oj), mean(vc)))
## 0.5 mg/day: OJ mean 13.23; VC mean 7.98
cat(sprintf("OJ - VC 95%% CI: %.2f to %.2f; p = %.4f", test$conf.int[1], test$conf.int[2], test$p.value))
## OJ - VC 95% CI: 1.72 to 8.78; p = 0.0064

The interactive chart shows each animal and the two group-mean curves. Hover details help users connect summary values to the observations.

A simple, transparent workflow

Choose · inspect · interpret

  1. Select OJ or VC.
  2. Choose a dose: 0.5, 1, or 2 mg/day.
  3. Read the mean and 95% interval; compare the other supplement at the same dose.
  4. Hover over chart points to inspect individual measurements.

Built-in help: the app contains usage instructions, definitions, and assumptions in its left-hand panel.

Limits: each group has 10 animals. The intervals assume independent observations and approximately normal measurements within groups. Results describe this experiment; they do not prove effects for all animals or doses.

Run it: shiny::runApp("toothgrowth_shiny_app")
